\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Key Information Extraction (KIE) on Visually Rich Documents (VRDs)—such as administrative forms, commercial invoices, and retail receipts—aims to map a raw page image and OCR-detected subword tokens with 2D bounding boxes into structured, typed entity spans for downstream enterprise automation. Although multimodal Transformers like LayoutLMv3 jointly encode text, 2D coordinates, and visual patch tokens, standard token-level fine-tuning exhibits four structural failure modes: (1) cross-column coordinate ambiguity from flat $(x_0, y_0, x_1, y_1)$ embeddings, (2) intra-segment label jitter and long-range key-value disconnection caused by isolated subword classification, (3) intra-line boundary bleeding where adjacent key and value spans on the same horizontal line merge into a single entity, and (4) cross-modal representation drift and background noise sensitivity under scanning artifacts or watermarks.

To resolve these bottlenecks without pre-training from scratch, I propose a structure-aware LayoutLMv3 architecture integrating four synergistic components: Hierarchical Position Encoding (HPE), a Segment Context Module (SCM), an Intra-Line Boundary Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{bound}}$), and a calibrated Cross-Modal Contrastive Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{cont}}$). HPE clusters word boxes into reading lines, vertical columns, and local blocks to inject explicit layout topology prior to self-attention. SCM mean-pools token states into line-level segment vectors, models inter-segment reading context via a ReZero-gated Transformer layer, and broadcasts segment consensus back to constituent tokens alongside a segment-onset indicator embedding. During training, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{bound}}$ supervises adjacent intra-line token pairs to enforce sharp entity transitions, while $\mathcal{L}_{\text{cont}}$ aligns pre-SCM token states with detached visual patch anchors via spatial-overlap soft-target InfoNCE, eliminating cross-modal gradient interference and incurring zero inference overhead.

I evaluate my framework across three random seeds ($\{42, 123, 1993\}$) on the FUNSD and CORD benchmarks using an NVIDIA A100 GPU. On FUNSD, my full 4-module architecture achieves an entity-level F1 score of $92.12 \pm 0.14\%$ on \texttt{base} ($+2.20\%$ over the $89.92 \pm 0.85\%$ baseline) and $93.35 \pm 0.18\%$ on \texttt{large} ($+2.11\%$ over the $91.24 \pm 0.61\%$ baseline), while reducing span boundary errors by $82.6\%$. On CORD, my model attains $97.28 \pm 0.12\%$ F1 on \texttt{base} and $97.62 \pm 0.08\%$ F1 on \texttt{large}. Systematic leave-one-out ablations confirm that every proposed module improves over the baseline, with SCM providing the largest single performance contribution.
