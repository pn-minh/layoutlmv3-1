\chapter*{Preface}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Preface}

Automating data entry from paper forms, invoices, and sales receipts has become a practical necessity in fields like finance, healthcare, and logistics. Every day, organizations process thousands of documents that contain important numbers, names, and dates. Manually typing these values into databases is slow, expensive, and often introduces typing errors. In recent years, machine learning models have made significant progress in automating this task, known as Key Information Extraction (KIE) on Visually Rich Documents (VRDs).

Unlike regular text documents, visually rich documents depend heavily on their visual and geometric layout. A price in a receipt gets its meaning from its row position and the item description to its left. Similarly, on an official form, question labels and their answer fields are arranged in columns or boxes. Models like LayoutLMv3 combine text, bounding box coordinates, and visual image patches into a unified Transformer. This design works well on general benchmarks, but several structural issues become apparent when fine-tuning the model on real forms and receipts:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Flat spatial coordinates:} The model embeds coordinates $(x_0, y_0, x_1, y_1)$ as separate numbers without explicit awareness of reading lines, text columns, or semantic blocks.
    \item \textbf{Token-level fragmentation:} Classifying each subword token independently often causes inconsistent predictions within the same multi-word entity.
    \item \textbf{Boundary ambiguity on the same line:} When different entity fields appear on the same horizontal line, such as a field name right next to its value, the model often merges them into a single entity.
\end{enumerate}

To address these issues, this graduation thesis investigates practical structural enhancements for LayoutLMv3. We develop an enhanced architecture that incorporates Hierarchical Position Encoding (HPE), a Segment Context Module (SCM) with a ReZero residual gate, an Intra-Line Boundary Loss, and a cross-modal Contrastive Loss. These components improve the model's structural awareness and entity consistency without requiring costly pre-training from scratch.

The thesis is organized into five chapters:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Chapter 1 (Introduction):} Introduces the problem of document key information extraction, explains the challenges found in existing models, and summarizes the main contributions of this work.
    \item \textbf{Chapter 2 (Background and Related Work):} Discusses foundational concepts including multimodal Transformers, sequence labeling with BIO tags, the evolution of the LayoutLM family, and related research on layout-aware models.
    \item \textbf{Chapter 3 (Proposed Methodology):} Details the architecture and equations for Hierarchical Position Encoding, the Segment Context Module, the Intra-Line Boundary Loss, and the Contrastive Loss.
    \item \textbf{Chapter 4 (Experiments and Results):} Describes our experimental setup on an NVIDIA A100 GPU, provides detailed statistics and visualizations of the FUNSD and CORD datasets, and presents multi-seed results and ablation studies.
    \item \textbf{Chapter 5 (Conclusion):} Summarizes the findings of this thesis, discusses current limitations, and outlines directions for future exploration.
\end{itemize}
