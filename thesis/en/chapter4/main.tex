\subsection{FUNSD Dataset}
The FUNSD dataset consists of 199 real scanned administrative and questionnaire forms sampled from the RVL-CDIP collection. The data is partitioned into 149 training images and 50 test images. In total, FUNSD includes 9,707 annotated semantic entities comprising 31,485 individual words.

Each word in FUNSD is assigned one of four functional roles:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{HEADER}: Titles, section banners, or institutional headers (e.g., "Registration Form", "Mailing Instructions").
    \item \texttt{QUESTION}: Field labels that solicit user entry (e.g., "Full Name:", "Telephone Number:", "Date of Birth:").
    \item \texttt{ANSWER}: Filled information corresponding to a question (e.g., "Pham Nhat Minh", "12/10/1998").
    \item \texttt{OTHER}: Incidental text that does not belong to the primary fields (e.g., boilerplate disclaimers, stamps, barcodes).
\end{itemize}
Under the IOB2 scheme, the three active categories yield $2 \times 3 + 1 = 7$ distinct classification tags.

\subsection{CORD Dataset}
The CORD dataset contains 1,000 shopping and restaurant receipts collected in Indonesia. The dataset is split into 800 training receipts, 100 validation receipts, and 100 test receipts. Due to mobile capture and thermal printer variations, receipt images in CORD often feature crumpled paper, skewed lines, and uneven lighting.

CORD defines 30 fine-grained semantic categories organized into 5 major groups:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{menu}: Information about ordered items, such as item name (\texttt{menu.nm}), unit price (\texttt{menu.unitprice}), item quantity (\texttt{menu.cnt}), and subtotal line price (\texttt{menu.price}).
    \item \texttt{total}: Summary fields for the transaction, such as total price (\texttt{total.total\_price}), cash paid (\texttt{total.cashprice}), and change returned (\texttt{total.changeprice}).
    \item \texttt{sub\_total}: Intermediate sums including subtotal before tax (\texttt{sub\_total.\allowbreak subtotal\_price}), tax (\texttt{sub\_total.tax\_price}), and service charges (\texttt{sub\_total.\allowbreak service\_price}).
    \item \texttt{void\_menu}: Canceled or discounted items.
    \item \texttt{etc}: Metadata including store names, tax numbers, and timestamp information.
\end{itemize}
With 30 active classes, the IOB2 label space contains $2 \times 30 + 1 = 61$ tags.

\section{Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:exp_metrics}

Following standard benchmarks \cite{huang2022layoutlmv3}, we report entity-level Precision ($P$), Recall ($R$), and F1 score computed using the \texttt{seqeval} library. An entity is counted as a True Positive ($\text{TP}$) only when its predicted token span and semantic class match the ground-truth annotation exactly. If a model predicts the correct entity category but truncates or extends the word boundaries by even a single token, that prediction is penalized as both a False Positive and a False Negative.

Precision, Recall, and F1 are defined as:
\begin{equation}
    P = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FP}}, \quad
    R = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FN}}, \quad
    F1 = \frac{2 \cdot P \cdot R}{P + R}.
\end{equation}

To avoid relying on a lucky single run, we evaluate every configuration across three independent random seeds: $42$, $123$, and $1993$. We report the mean and sample standard deviation ($\text{Mean} \pm \text{Std}$) for all metrics.

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:main_results} and Figure~\ref{fig:main_results_f1_comparison}, our proposed architectural enhancements deliver substantial performance and stability gains:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Complex Document Understanding (FUNSD):} On the challenging FUNSD benchmark characterized by irregular multi-column forms and dense tabular fields, our 3-module model achieves \textbf{91.58\%} F1 on \texttt{base} ($\mathbf{+1.66\%}$ over baseline), with Precision surging by $\mathbf{+2.50\%}$ ($89.32\% \to 91.82\%$). On \texttt{large}, the Full 4-Module Model (+ Cross-Modal Contrastive Loss) delivers the highest overall score of \textbf{92.92\%} F1, outperforming the baseline by $\mathbf{+1.68\%}$ and beating the 3-module model by $\mathbf{+0.47\%}$. This demonstrates that when parameter capacity is sufficient, contrastive cross-modal alignment grounds text embeddings effectively into visual layout patches.
    \item \textbf{Receipt Key Information Extraction (CORD):} On CORD receipts where the vanilla baseline is already near saturation ($\sim 96.7\% - 97.1\%$), our 3-module architecture consistently outperforms the baseline, reaching \textbf{96.91\%} on \texttt{base} ($\mathbf{+0.19\%}$) and \textbf{97.35\%} on \texttt{large} ($\mathbf{+0.20\%}$). The full 4-module model achieves high prediction stability on CORD \texttt{base}, with standard deviation dropping to just $\pm 0.06\%$.
    \item \textbf{Drastic Variance Reduction:} Across all experimental runs, our enhanced architectures exhibit superior optimization stability compared to the vanilla baseline. On FUNSD \texttt{base}, the baseline standard deviation of $\pm 0.85\%$ is reduced to $\pm 0.16\%$ in the 3-module model (a $5.3\times$ variance reduction). On CORD \texttt{large}, variance drops from $\pm 0.17\%$ down to an exceptionally stable $\pm 0.04\%$.
\end{enumerate}

Key architectural insights from our systematic ablation include:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Token-Segment SCM is the Primary Driver of Entity Consistency:}
    Across all four benchmark configurations, eliminating the Token-Segment SCM causes the most severe degradation in F1: $-1.22\%$ on FUNSD \texttt{base}, $-1.44\%$ on FUNSD \texttt{large}, $-0.32\%$ on CORD \texttt{base}, and $-0.51\%$ on CORD \texttt{large}. This confirms that token-level self-attention alone struggles with intra-line semantic fragmentation; explicitly aggregating tokens into segment representations via bi-directional gating enforces contextual homogeneity across whole semantic phrases.
    
    \item \textbf{Cross-Modal Contrastive Loss Scales with Model Capacity and Layout Complexity:}
    On FUNSD \texttt{large}, adding Cross-Modal Contrastive Loss yields a decisive gain of $\mathbf{+0.47\%}$ over the 3-module model ($92.45\% \to 92.92\%$). The 24-layer backbone has sufficient capacity to learn a rich joint metric space that aligns visual features with text embeddings without interfering with classification heads. On simpler or near-ceiling receipt benchmarks (CORD), where visual image patches consist largely of crumpled textures and skewed thermal print, contrastive loss acts as a heavy regularizer that stabilizes variance ($\pm 0.06\%$) while maintaining high overall accuracy.

    \item \textbf{Intra-Line Boundary Loss Prevents Multi-Token Fragmentation:}
    Removing $\mathcal{L}_{\text{bound}}$ drops large-model performance by $-0.30\%$ on FUNSD and $-0.34\%$ on CORD. Supervised penalty on adjacent transition matrices forces the classifier to sharply delineate entity boundaries, preventing multi-word entities from bleeding into surrounding fields.

    \item \textbf{Hierarchical Position Encoding (HPE) Anchors Spatial Layout:}
    On CORD \texttt{large}, omitting HPE drops performance from $97.35\%$ to $96.91\%$ (a $-0.44\%$ drop). Decomposing token coordinates into line, column, and block embeddings resolves the ambiguity of 1D serialized sequence order in narrow, multi-tier receipt layouts.
\end{enumerate}
