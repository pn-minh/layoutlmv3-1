\subsection{FUNSD Dataset}
The FUNSD dataset consists of 199 real scanned administrative and questionnaire forms sampled from the RVL-CDIP collection. The data is partitioned into 149 training images and 50 test images. In total, FUNSD includes 9,707 annotated semantic entities comprising 31,485 individual words.

Each word in FUNSD is assigned one of four functional roles:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{HEADER}: Titles, section banners, or institutional headers (e.g., "Registration Form", "Mailing Instructions").
    \item \texttt{QUESTION}: Field labels that solicit user entry (e.g., "Full Name:", "Telephone Number:", "Date of Birth:").
    \item \texttt{ANSWER}: Filled information corresponding to a question (e.g., "Pham Nhat Minh", "12/10/1998").
    \item \texttt{OTHER}: Incidental text that does not belong to the primary fields (e.g., boilerplate disclaimers, stamps, barcodes).
\end{itemize}
Under the IOB2 scheme, the three active categories yield $2 \times 3 + 1 = 7$ distinct classification tags.

\subsection{CORD Dataset}
The CORD dataset contains 1,000 shopping and restaurant receipts collected in Indonesia. The dataset is split into 800 training receipts, 100 validation receipts, and 100 test receipts. Due to mobile capture and thermal printer variations, receipt images in CORD often feature crumpled paper, skewed lines, and uneven lighting.

CORD defines 30 fine-grained semantic categories organized into 5 major groups:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{menu}: Information about ordered items, such as item name (\texttt{menu.nm}), unit price (\texttt{menu.unitprice}), item quantity (\texttt{menu.cnt}), and subtotal line price (\texttt{menu.price}).
    \item \texttt{total}: Summary fields for the transaction, such as total price (\texttt{total.total\_price}), cash paid (\texttt{total.cashprice}), and change returned (\texttt{total.changeprice}).
    \item \texttt{sub\_total}: Intermediate sums including subtotal before tax (\texttt{sub\_total.\allowbreak subtotal\_price}), tax (\texttt{sub\_total.tax\_price}), and service charges (\texttt{sub\_total.\allowbreak service\_price}).
    \item \texttt{void\_menu}: Canceled or discounted items.
    \item \texttt{etc}: Metadata including store names, tax numbers, and timestamp information.
\end{itemize}
With 30 active classes, the IOB2 label space contains $2 \times 30 + 1 = 61$ tags.

\section{Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:exp_metrics}

Following standard benchmarks \cite{huang2022layoutlmv3}, we report entity-level Precision ($P$), Recall ($R$), and F1 score computed using the \texttt{seqeval} library. An entity is counted as a True Positive ($\text{TP}$) only when its predicted token span and semantic class match the ground-truth annotation exactly. If a model predicts the correct entity category but truncates or extends the word boundaries by even a single token, that prediction is penalized as both a False Positive and a False Negative.

Precision, Recall, and F1 are defined as:
\begin{equation}
    P = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FP}}, \quad
    R = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FN}}, \quad
    F1 = \frac{2 \cdot P \cdot R}{P + R}.
\end{equation}

To avoid relying on a lucky single run, we evaluate every configuration across three independent random seeds: $42$, $123$, and $1993$. We report the mean and sample standard deviation ($\text{Mean} \pm \text{Std}$) for all metrics.

On the FUNSD benchmark at \texttt{base} scale, our proposed model achieves a mean F1 score of \textbf{91.58\%}, improving over the baseline (89.92\%) by \textbf{+1.66\%}. The primary improvement comes from Precision, which increases by \textbf{+2.50\%} (from 89.32\% to 91.82\%). On CORD, where the baseline is already very strong at $96.72\%$, the proposed model consistently improves precision to $96.91\%$ on \texttt{base} and reaches $97.35\%$ on \texttt{large}.

The ablation results show that the Segment Context Module provides the largest single performance gain. Removing SCM causes the F1 score to drop from 91.58\% to 90.36\% (a loss of $-1.22\%$). This indicates that pooling tokens into segment representations and sharing context across lines is the primary driver of entity consistency.

Removing the Intra-Line Boundary Loss decreases the F1 score to 91.18\%, demonstrating that supervising transitions between adjacent tokens helps sharpen entity boundaries. Omitting HPE or Contrastive Loss also produces measurable drops (down to 91.33\% and 91.36\%), confirming that explicit spatial layout signals and cross-modal alignment offer complementary benefits.
